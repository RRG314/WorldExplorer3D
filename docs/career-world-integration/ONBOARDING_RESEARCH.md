# Onboarding evidence and application

Sources reviewed November 4–5, 2026. Primary research supports testable principles, not a guarantee that a redesign improves retention. The current audit is developer-informed automation, so new-player comprehension remains a future human study.

## Research

[Andersen et al., CHI 2012, The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity](https://centerforgamescience.org/files/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf) compared tutorial designs in three games with more than 45,000 participants. Effects varied with complexity; restrictive guidance did not show a universal benefit, and on-demand help was not uniformly beneficial. Its games, self-selected participants and text/image tutorials limit generalization. **Application inference:** give World Explorer a short real action, optional help and room to experiment; test each mechanic instead of applying one compulsory wizard everywhere.

[Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf) examines autonomy, competence and relatedness in game motivation. **Application inference:** preserve free exploration, make one achievable outcome understandable, and support meaningful shared activity. It is not causal evidence that a focus menu will improve this app’s D7 retention.

## Product hypotheses to test

H1: one clear current action reduces direction confusion versus Today’s competing sections. H2: choosing interest after a real saved result helps novices understand the choice. H3: optional early choice serves motivated specialists without blocking everyone else. H4: showing owned/provided tools before shops prevents unnecessary purchase confusion. H5: a result→Journal→next-choice loop makes progress more understandable. H6: explicit help stays useful without recurring ambient banners.

Study design: first moderated pilot across mixed experience and access needs; compare tasks, not cosmetic preference alone. Observe actual movement, valid task choice, meaningful result, Journal review and return after interruption. Record intervention/error/abandonment and ask what a career changes and where data is saved. Start with 6–8 sessions as a diagnostic pilot, not statistically conclusive retention research. Change/expand the sample based on findings. Obtain appropriate consent; do not recruit, record or contact anyone automatically.

Later opt-in telemetry may compare completion/resume/voluntary next activity and D1/D7, with geography/error context separated from voluntary opt-out. Do not log raw location trails, private media or entire Journal histories. Numeric targets in future gates are product goals, not literature-derived guarantees.
