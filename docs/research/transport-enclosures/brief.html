Bridges and tunnels need connected space—not overlapping strips.

Two underlying problems explain the recurring failures: uncertain map labels were helping decide which roads connect, and separate tunnel meshes were pretending to be one interior.

Local implementation checkpoint. The shared tunnel boundary and camera-body protections are implemented. Portal fit, broader bridge coverage and production acceptance remain open. Nothing has been deployed.

What the map can—and cannot—tell us

Shortbread is a lean cartographic schema. Its covered-road indication combines tunnels, building passages and covered roads; street names live in a separate label layer. It does not guarantee surveyed clearance, elevation or original road-node topology. A nearby label is useful for the HUD, but it is not proof that two roads connect. Shortbread schema 1.0.

OSM layers indicate relative stacking, not a height in meters. Tunnel boundaries and internal way splits also serve different purposes: a data seam is not automatically a portal. The implementation must distinguish missing data from explicit evidence. OSM layer guidance; OSM tunnel guidance.

The architectural change

A junction should have one owned boundary and a consistent height contract. ASAM's road-network specification formalizes closed junction boundaries and a shared junction elevation surface. That is a useful architectural precedent, not a claim that World Explorer implements or is certified against OpenDRIVE. ASAM OpenDRIVE 1.8.1, junction boundaries; junction elevation grids.

  1. Establish supported connections. Retain source identities and layer evidence. Inferred display names no longer create route-gap connections.
  2. Compile the connected clearance volume. Indexed closed sweeps share their station vertices. A locally bundled Manifold worker unions compatible connected mouths, removes internal surfaces and releases its temporary objects.
  3. Publish one result. The resulting boundary supplies the lining, wall collision and camera/ceiling queries. Successful components do not draw the old independent shells underneath.
  4. Protect the view after collision response. A shortened camera boom must stay outside the attached vehicle's actual visual bounds. Prefer a clear roof pose; use temporary first person only where an exterior pose cannot fit.

Closed-mesh Boolean operations require valid inputs; simply deleting wall intervals does not construct a joined roof. Manifold supplies the browser geometry operation, not geographical truth or automatic performance guarantees. CGAL 5.5 mesh-processing manual; Manifold API documentation (runtime pinned to 3.5.3).

What execution showed

The first prototype passed simple fixtures but left internal cap slivers in an oblique, graded Monaco tunnel. Driving revealed the failure. A targeted regression now checks that case; one indexed sweep replaces the defective chain of separately hulled pieces.

On the fresh Monaco fallback run, seven connected components compiled in approximately 307–466 ms on this desktop. Rear-chase, wall-contact, moving-junction and narrow-layout images were inspected. The BMW is no longer viewed through its cabin in the reproduced wall-contact case. These are local observations, not a worldwide or phone-performance guarantee.

What remains before release

Portal collars and terrain openings still need to fit the accepted ground. Terrain holes alone do not supply replacement geometry or collision agreement. Unity 2022.3 terrain-hole guidance illustrates that separation.

Exact-provider paths, additional bridge transitions, extreme sweep shapes, bounded-worker failures, full packaged delivery and physical-phone performance remain acceptance work. The source data cannot support a promise of surveyed accuracy everywhere. No building should be removed to disguise a conflict.
