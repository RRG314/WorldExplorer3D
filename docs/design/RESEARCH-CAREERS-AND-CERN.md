# Research careers, working places and Collision Lab integration

October 10, 2026. Extends `EXPLORER-CAREER-PLAN.md`; it does not replace the
existing save, movement, geographic, property, marine or space authorities.
The owner requested broad research and exploration careers, mining, real objects
and better assets, and reuse of the existing **lhc game / Collision Lab** project.
This document is a delivery specification. A described mission is not a shipped
feature. The delivery ledger records implementation and acceptance separately.

## One opening, several complete journeys

The first choice is **what would you like to investigate?** Choose a career or
free roam. A career is a recommended sequence, not a permanent character class.
It does not reset a character background, remove tools, require starting a new
save, or prevent joining another career. The existing character specialties keep
owning skill XP; Journal receipts keep owning accomplishments. No second XP bar.

Today shows the selected investigation, its next physical action, the target and
why it matters. The Travel map offers admitted destinations for that action.
Journal shows the actual observations and completed report. Backpack contains
physical items; observation records and analysis reports are not pretend loot.
H01 teaches recording and reviewing one result. It is adapted to the selected
career when a suitable target exists. A CERN or ocean starter should eventually
teach that lesson at its own working location, without requiring a random city
collection first. Existing H01 completions remain valid.

For a new player, the first job must take 5–10 minutes excluding loading, have
one understandable question and show a visible result. Start with a short action,
then a comparison, then a small expedition, then a decision supported by evidence.
Durations are design targets, not measured promises. Advanced assignments may
combine careers; no basic career should require completing every other one.

Each offer requires: a loaded reachable location, actual interactable objects,
the necessary equipment, a compatible evidence method and an available return.
If any requirement is missing, show a specific unavailable reason and an actual
alternative. A catalog row, texture reference or model file is not admission.

## Career campaigns and tangible results

Each sequence below is a finite first campaign. Its numbered stages are authored
assignments with different decisions, not names pasted over the same timer.
Optional repeatable work follows the completed sequence; it never extends the
first campaign indefinitely. Reports permit supported, partial and inconclusive
results. Choosing “need more evidence” can be correct and must not punish honesty.

## Concrete source findings, not assumed features

- Character specialties already include geology, marine, space, wildlife,
  piloting, engineering, photography and surveying. Career selection should point
  at these; archaeology/mining/botany can initially share relevant skill practice
  without inventing competing levels. Career completion is a separate receipt.
- The field plan deliberately admits **mapped-geology-study** for geology. It
  does not place exposed rock scans or guarantee collectible rocks at that point.
  Existing geology guidance cannot satisfy the outcrop/sample campaign by itself.
- Current field equipment is mostly procedural mesh construction in
  `discovery/field-equipment.js`. Existing tool capability does not certify its
  art quality. Replace hero tools through the shared model/lease catalog and keep
  grip, interaction point, scale and collision separate from art hierarchy.
- `field-expedition.js` currently selects three varied nearby activities. Keep
  its short optional outing, but do not treat this generic list as a professional
  career. Authored careers need target binding, ordered evidence and interpretation.
- Mining has material lots and commerce support, but no audited quarry extraction
  loop. Archaeology has a virtual field activity, not a complete research site.
- Housing and Backpack were separate unchecked local writes. A single durable
  possessions document and Journal/custody reconciliation are prerequisites for
  using sample storage as a campaign reward.

## Reuse the existing Collision Lab

A separate local Collision Lab reference project uses package `collision-lab`
**0.7.0** and Three.js **0.186.0**. Its source is maintained separately from this
repository; importing it requires a reviewed integration.

Reuse candidates verified in source:

- `src/missions.mjs`: seven structured missions with two-to-five cases, goals,
  controls, explanations, scientific limits and source links.
- `src/models.mjs`, `science.mjs`, `particle-analysis.mjs`, `receipt.mjs`:
  numerical experiments, event interpretation and reproducibility validation.
- `data-integrity.mjs`: pinned CMS bundle digest. Recorded events are distinct
  from model-generated hits and hypothetical events.
- Beam, ring, transport, reconstruction, theory, generator and published-analysis
  workers, each with its own model/data limits and archive contracts. Load the
  selected experiment only; the vendor directory is about **188 MB**, not an
  acceptable addition to the normal city load.
- `public/geometry`: about **13 MB** of CMS iSpy detector-display GLBs with source
  commit and SHA-256 inventory; MIT notices retained. These are display geometry,
  not a complete architectural/CAD twin of CERN. The concrete material has CC0
  provenance and real-world scale metadata.
- `facility.mjs` owns an orbit camera, renderer and animation loop. It is **not**
  a player collision/navigation controller, and it uses a different Three release.
  Its tunnel, ring, bench and hall are four alternative scene groups with local
  origins, not four connected rooms. Importing that entire scene into the r128
  world would duplicate rendering, lifecycle and controls. Reuse geometry and
  numerical contracts, then author actual site transforms and circulation.

### Facility layout and playable route

Represent the real Meyrin campus/Science Gateway visitor context from reviewed
geography. CERN facilities occupy different sites: do not place CMS's detector
beside ATLAS as though they were rooms of one building. The CMS destination is a
separate admitted site reached through Travel/transport. Surface buildings,
entrances, shaft/elevator, service circulation and detector viewing areas need
reviewed references and meter-scale dimensions. Unknown rooms are labeled
illustrative training spaces; don't call a guessed floor plan an exact replica.

The initial complete visit is exterior arrival → reception/map → training
beamline → detector viewing route → analysis room → report display → exterior
return. Every doorway, elevator stop, stair/alternative route and exit must be
walkable in both directions with the existing character/camera. No orbit-camera
teleport counts as traversal. Physical barriers separate the avatar from
machine volumes; changing an experiment cannot put the player inside a magnet.
A cutaway is a deliberate exhibit control, not missing walls.

Training stations own distinct interactions: steer field, tune RF phase, compare
focus planes, open detector readouts, select/fix hits, inspect an actual recorded
event and compare populations. Controls appear only at an active station, in the
existing compact panel; close returns full walking control. Each station has a
visible instrument/readout and a physical location. Keyboard and touch offer the
same numerical result, repeat/retry behavior and explanation.

### Integration boundary

1. Preserve a reviewed source snapshot/hash list; select only needed original
   modules, data and notices. Original Collision Lab code is GPL-3.0-or-later;
   World Explorer is source-available. Before distribution choose a compatible
   packaging/license arrangement for original code and retain all third-party
   terms. Do not silently strip licenses or copy an entire vendor tree.
2. Extract the pure numerical/receipt API behind a small versioned experiment
   adapter. Do not import another THREE package into World Explorer. The existing
   shared renderer, model cache and runtime scheduler present the physical lab.
3. Worker request: experiment ID/version, bounded inputs, seed and source digest.
   Response: units, result, diagnostics, evidence category, reproducibility inputs.
   Reject malformed results and stale/aborted sessions; terminate on exit.
4. Journal transaction records a verified personal result with an idempotent
   mission/case identity. Existing Collision Lab notebooks remain importable only
   after recomputation/validation; an arbitrary posted “passed” message grants
   nothing. No shared currency or competitive proof comes from client results.
5. Advanced workbenches open at admitted stations on demand. Pause/release the
   previous worker and large buffers before another is loaded. Remain usable when
   optional upstream datasets are unavailable; keep completed reports exportable.
6. Build and review the physical site before advertising “explore CERN.” Reusing
   a numerical experiment is valuable, but it does not finish the destination.

## Asset and object acceptance

Every authored objective resolves a stable target instance to: source feature or
fictional training-site identity, visual asset version/hash/license, transform in
meters, collision/walk surface, interaction anchors, tool verbs, evidence method,
LOD/residency cost and teardown owner. Asset presence alone is insufficient.

Hero objects require readable geometry, coherent PBR channels, believable wear,
correct proportions, working joints where used, intentional grip/interaction
points and a matched palette. Inspect the real rendered asset at player height,
in sun, shade and task lighting. Asset thumbnails are not acceptance. Maps should
look like mapped places; a rock-study marker cannot substitute for an outcrop.

Quarry needs visible geology, assay stations, functioning drill/cutter, ore/waste
containers, vehicle/loading route and restoration surfaces. Archaeology needs
layers, grid, tools, meaningful artifacts, packing and catalog bench. Ocean
careers need the completed ship, launch/recovery, working instruments, layered
underwater habitat and animated animals. CERN needs correct detector geometry,
building/circulation art, beamline machinery, control/readout furniture and usable
stations. Each missing item stays on the admission checklist until integrated.

Target capture uses bounded detail, shared textures, authored collision proxies
and demand loading. Keep the whole world covered. Asset upgrades must not alter
road/bridge/tunnel surface heights or replace an environment's movement authority.
Before collecting a new asset set, validate one complete interactive specimen of
that class in the actual game and measure the resident memory/draw-call cost.

1. Finish the atomic home/Backpack prerequisite with failure/reload/two-tab checks.
2. Replace generic opening guidance with an existing-panel career choice and a
   physically available first lead; persist selected intent in the same profile.
   Do not add disabled “coming soon” missions to the main objective feed.
3. Finish H02–H05 and one complete terrestrial research campaign. Admit additional
   campaigns only after their actual targets and all five stages work. Use the
   same mission lifecycle and Journal commit boundary for each, with distinct
   domain methods and authored decisions.
4. Finish vessel/underwater and planetary spines, then their career campaigns.
5. Integrate the Collision Lab numerical seam, validate unchanged reference
   outputs, then finish the physical CERN destination and station journey.
6. Complete mining extraction, material custody and restoration as one playable
   quarry; expand to regional and planetary deposits only after this closes.
7. Complete remaining research campaigns, reviewed GPS variants and co-op roles.
   GPS/public access and shared economic actions retain their stronger authorities.
8. Execute realism and release gates from the parent plans, using actual career
   routes as repeatable scenes. No blanket “production ready” from unit tests.

Each admitted campaign requires: clean first launch, normal completion, wrong
method/target rejection, pause/resume, abandoned/replaced target, partial return,
failed save/retry, exact evidence review, idempotent reward, backup/restore,
older-save preservation, cleanup after environment changes, desktop/touch layout
and a real physical-control journey. Compare reports with actual inputs; check
asset appearance and collision independently. Record scripted positioning as
setup, never as proof that a new player can find their way.

## R&D and design implications

These sources informed design decisions; the game adaptations above are ours.

- [USGS field mapping](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1451/haugerud/index.html)
  supports station/sample identity and separating observations from interpreted
  map units. [USGS resource assessment](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/mineral-resources-program/assessing-mineral-resources)
  supports geology-led prospecting rather than scattering random ore everywhere.
- [NOAA map and dive](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/expedition-feature/map-dive-exploration-nots/)
  links seafloor mapping to site selection. [NOAA CTD](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/ctd/)
  supplies the distinct water-column measurement loop.
- [NPS archaeological documentation](https://home.nps.gov/articles/sec-standards-archeo-doc-guidelines.htm)
  informs contextual survey/interpretation. [NPS visit guidance](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/archeology/guidelines-for-visiting.htm)
  supports observation-only treatment of real heritage locations.
- [Cornell eBird protocols](https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48000950859)
  informs effort-aware stationary/traveling wildlife observations.
  [NPS phenology](https://www.nps.gov/articles/greatsmokiesphenology.htm)
  supports repeat visits and seasonal comparison for botany.
- [NASA sampling sequence](https://science.nasa.gov/blog/sampling-strategy-for-the-delta-front-campaign/)
  informs observation, selected sampling and comparison on planetary missions.
- [CMS education data guide](https://opendata.cern.ch/docs/cms-guide-for-education)
  supports recorded-event inspection and analysis. [CMS virtual visits](https://cms.cern/interact-with-cms/virtual-visits)
  provides facility context; it does not grant an exact architectural model.


## Local implementation and reuse qualification — October 10

The opening chapter now stores a chosen field assignment in its existing Journal
profile. Its lead must be a real, unclaimed, released field slot with an available
tool; choosing a specialty does not invent objects, bypass access or create a
second XP system. Other kinds of fieldwork remain available in free roam, but
only the chosen method advances this opening assignment. An observation career
does not require collecting a specimen to continue its regional survey. This is
the introductory assignment, **not completion of the full careers above**.

Current starter definitions cover geology, wildlife, botany, palaeontology and
habitat surveying, plus general exploration. The actual menu admits only choices
with eligible local work and current tool access. Existing advanced-tool rank
requirements still apply. Mining and CERN are not advertised as playable yet.

The in-place Collision Lab qualification passed ten checks using its actual
numerical modules, not rewritten equivalents: steering/field polarity, RF energy,
reachable focusing cases, distinguishing detector signals, fitted versus unrelated
hits, Lorentz-frame invariance, all 100,000 recorded rows, charge-control selection,
reproducible receipts and tamper rejection, and the authored mission definitions.
All 18 detector binaries match their source manifest and retained MIT notice.
Run `node scripts/verification/collision-lab-reuse.mjs <existing-project-path>`
to repeat. No source, scientific vendor tree or user notebook was copied or edited.

These checks establish a reusable numerical/data baseline. They do not verify
world traversal, architectural accuracy, visual realism, accessibility, server
rewards or browser performance. The source geometry remains event-display data;
a realistic, walkable CERN destination still requires the facility and asset work
specified above. The original app's renderer and orbit controls are not imported
into World Explorer.


A disposable source-browser inspection also opened the existing tunnel,
training apparatus and CMS hall, loaded the detector without geometry errors,
and checked the 390-pixel layout without horizontal overflow. The images show
usable diagram geometry and a basic illustrated hall; they do not meet the
requested realistic-world art bar. Detector cutaways are educational controls,
not real traversable holes. The review retained the source project's own files
and notebooks unchanged. The screenshot/metrics receipt is local under
`output/production-readiness-20261010/collision-lab-browser-final/`; its frame
averages include transitions and must not be reported as steady-state performance.
